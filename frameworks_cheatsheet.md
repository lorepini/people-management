# People Management — Frameworks Cheat Sheet

*Essay-ready. Six blocks, 87 frameworks. Cite by name + author + the move it gives you.*

## How to use

- 🔴 **Core** ★★★★★ — essay anchors. Every long answer should rest on at least one of these.
- 🔵 **Key** ★★★★ — strong supporting cites. Use to add nuance or a second voice.
- 🟣 **Supporting** ★★★ — exam-detail level. Drop in to show range.
- One-line italic under the name = the **one-breath argument** to lead with.
- Bullets = the **moves** (what to actually write in the essay).

# Block 1 · Foundations: SHRM, the HR system, engagement & commitment

### 🔴 Harvard Model of HRM · S2 · Beer, Spector, Lawrence, Mills & Walton (1984) ★★★★★
*A multi-stakeholder map: situational forces + stakeholder interests shape HR policy choices → HR outcomes → long-term well-being.*
- **Inputs** — situational factors (workforce, strategy, technology, laws, market) + stakeholder interests (shareholders, employees, unions, community, government)
- **Four HR policy areas** — employee influence, HR flow, reward systems, work systems
- **Four C's (HR outcomes)** — Commitment, Competence, Congruence, Cost-effectiveness
- **Long-term consequences** — individual well-being, organizational effectiveness, societal well-being
- **Exam move** — the lens to redesign whole HR systems (Ryanair); pair with [[multistakeholder]] critique of shareholder-only HR

### 🔴 AMO Model · S1 · Ability–Motivation–Opportunity ★★★★★
*Performance = f(Ability × Motivation × Opportunity). If any factor is zero, performance is zero.*
- **Ability** — built by HR Flow (recruit, select, train, develop)
- **Motivation** — built by Performance Management (goals, feedback, recognition)
- **Opportunity** — built by Compensation + work design (resources, structure, autonomy)
- **Exam move** — the spine of the whole course; diagnose any HR failure as an A, M or O gap

### 🔴 HR System / Employee Journey · S1 · S2 ★★★★★
*HRM = three activity blocks staged across the employee lifecycle.*
- **HR Flow** — planning → recruitment → selection → onboarding → development → exit
- **Performance Management** — set goals, measure, feedback, develop
- **Compensation** — base pay, incentives, benefits, recognition
- **Lifecycle frame** — Recruit & Hire → Onboard → Engage → Develop → Progress → Exit
- **Exam move** — organizing backbone; structure any "design an HR system" answer along this arc

### 🔴 Total Motivation (ToMo) · S3 · Doshi & McGregor (HBR) ★★★★★
*Six why-people-work motives; the direct three lift performance, the indirect three destroy it.*
- **Direct (boost performance)** — Play, Purpose, Potential
- **Indirect (kill performance)** — Emotional pressure, Economic pressure, Inertia
- **ToMo score** — weighted index (-100 to +100), predicts customer outcomes
- **Exam move** — cite when explaining culture-as-performance-engine; contrast with extrinsic-only [[michigan-model]]

### 🔴 Work Engagement Model · S3 · Schaufeli & Bakker (2004) ★★★★★
*Engagement = a positive, fulfilling work-state with three dimensions.*
- **Vigor** — energy, resilience, willingness to invest effort
- **Dedication** — enthusiasm, inspiration, pride, significance
- **Absorption** — concentration, time-flies, fascination
- **Distinction** — engagement = state; commitment = attitude/bond
- **Exam move** — use to define engagement crisply before discussing drivers

### 🔴 Three-Component Commitment Model · S3 · Meyer & Allen (1991) ★★★★★
*Three reasons employees stay — only one is genuinely "good".*
- **Affective** — want to stay (emotional attachment) → best outcomes
- **Normative** — feel obliged to stay (loyalty/duty) → mixed
- **Continuance** — need to stay (cost of leaving) → worst outcomes
- **Exam move** — argue HRM should build affective commitment; continuance traps people

### 🔵 Michigan Model / HR Cycle · S2 · Fombrun, Tichy & Devanna (1984) ★★★★
*Hard, "matching" HRM: align mission/strategy/structure with a tight HR cycle for shareholder value.*
- **The cycle** — Selection → Appraisal → Rewards → Development
- **Logic** — instrumental, performance- and shareholder-driven
- **Exam move** — the foil to Harvard; "soft" Harvard vs "hard" Michigan is the classic essay debate

### 🔵 Self-Determination Theory · S3 · Deci & Ryan (1985) ★★★★
*Intrinsic motivation thrives when three needs are met.*
- **Autonomy** — control over how you work
- **Competence** — feeling effective at challenging tasks
- **Relatedness** — meaningful connection to others
- **Exam move** — theoretical scaffolding under [[tomo]] and engagement; cite to explain *why* practices work

### 🔵 Conservation of Resources (COR) · S3 · Hobfoll (1989) ★★★★
*People invest in, protect and grow valued resources; resource gains compound, losses spiral.*
- **Resource gain spiral** — high-commitment HR practices give resources, employees reinvest them
- **Resource loss spiral** — burnout, turnover when resources drain
- **Exam move** — explain how positive HR practices snowball into engagement

### 🔵 Social Exchange Theory · S3 · Cropanzano & Mitchell (2005) ★★★★
*Employer–employee relationship = reciprocal exchange of obligations and benefits.*
- **Norm of reciprocity** — give support, get effort back (and vice versa)
- **Exam move** — explains why perceived organizational support → engagement → discretionary effort

### 🔵 Multistakeholder HRM · S2 · Beer, Boselie & Brewster (2015) ★★★★
*HRM should serve shareholders, employees, unions, community AND government — not only shareholders.*
- **Critique** — RBV/strategic HRM has narrowed to firm-only outcomes
- **Argument** — balance economic, individual and societal well-being
- **Exam move** — the assigned reading's thesis; pair with [[harvard-model]] for the values argument

### 🔵 Perceived Organizational Support (POS) · S3 ★★★★
*Engagement/commitment rise when employees perceive the org cares about them.*
- **Antecedents** — job characteristics, organizational justice, recognition/rewards, leadership, psychological safety
- **Exam move** — identifies the HR levers that build engagement; concrete checklist for redesign

### 🔵 Strategic HRM: Vertical & Horizontal Fit · S2 ★★★★
*HRM = behaviour design serving business strategy; must be doubly aligned.*
- **Vertical fit** — HR practices match business strategy
- **Horizontal fit** — HR practices reinforce each other (no contradictions)
- **Exam move** — diagnose Ryanair: low-cost strategy → tight cost HR (horizontal fit); is it ethical (vertical critique)?

### 🟣 Unethical Pro-Organizational Behavior (UPB) · S3 ★★★
*Highly committed employees may lie/cheat to benefit the firm.*
- **Examples** — concealing defects, misleading customers, fudging numbers
- **Exam move** — the dark side of strong commitment; cite to balance the engagement narrative

### 🟣 Best Workplace on Earth (DREAMS) · S3 · Goffee & Jones (HBR 2013) ★★★
*Six attributes of the ideal organization to work for.*
- **Differences** — you can be yourself
- **Radical honesty** — you're told what's really going on
- **Extra value** — your strengths are magnified
- **Authenticity** — the company stands for something meaningful
- **Meaning** — daily work is rewarding
- **Simple rules** — no stupid rules
- **Exam move** — practical checklist for "what makes an engaging workplace"

### 🟣 Strategic Fitness Process · S2 · Beer & Eisenstat ★★★
*Structured intervention letting employees "speak truth to power" about strategy/values alignment.*
- **Mechanism** — surfaces the barriers to change that hierarchy hides
- **Exam move** — operationalizes the "employee voice" principle of Harvard

### 🟣 Resource-Based View / Human Capital · S2 ★★★
*People = sustained competitive advantage; invest in them like any other asset.*
- **Logic** — valuable, rare, inimitable, organized (VRIO) human capital wins
- **Exam move** — the universalist paradigm that Beer/Boselie/Brewster critique as too firm-centric

# Block 2 · Job design, analysis, competencies, workforce planning, recruit & select

### 🔴 Four Spans of Job Design · S4 · Simons (HBR) ★★★★★
*Design jobs so the supply of resources matches the demand on the role — an "X" of crossing sliders.*
- **Span of Control** — resources commanded (people, assets) → **supply**
- **Span of Support** — help available from others → **supply**
- **Span of Accountability** — range of metrics measured → **demand**
- **Span of Influence** — breadth of cross-unit cooperation needed → **demand**
- **Diagnostic** — sliders form an X when supply = demand; misalignment = crisis (resources / control / red tape)
- **Exam move** — the central Block-2 tool; diagnose any "broken role" by mapping the four sliders

### 🔴 Job Characteristics Model / MPS · S5 · Hackman & Oldham ★★★★★
*Five job features create three psychological states that drive motivation, satisfaction and effectiveness.*
- **Five core characteristics** — skill variety, task identity, task significance, autonomy, feedback
- **Three psychological states** — meaningfulness, responsibility, knowledge of results
- **MPS formula** — `((Variety + Identity + Significance) / 3) × Autonomy × Feedback`
- **Exam move** — use to argue *why* enrichment beats narrow specialization; assess fit for Ricci/Verona

### 🔴 Competency Model / KSAOs · S5 · Campion et al. (2011) ★★★★★
*A competency = the knowledge, skills, abilities and other characteristics needed for effective performance.*
- **K** — knowledge (what you know)
- **S** — skills (what you can do)
- **A** — abilities (cognitive/physical capacity)
- **O** — other (personality, motives, values)
- **Types** — technical competencies + leadership/behavioural competencies
- **Exam move** — the bedrock of the job description; drives selection criteria and gap analyses

### 🔴 Job Analysis: Job Description vs Job Specification · S5–S6 ★★★★★
*Systematic process splitting what the job is from what the holder must be.*
- **Job Description** — title, duties, responsibilities, reporting line, environment
- **Job Specification** — education, experience, skills, KSAOs, personal characteristics
- **Exam move** — starting point for recruitment; without it, selection has no criteria

### 🔴 Workforce Planning · S6 · Demand vs Supply ★★★★★
*Right person, right job, right time, right cost — by balancing labor demand and labor supply.*
- **Step 1** — analyse current workforce
- **Step 2** — forecast future demand (strategy-driven)
- **Step 3** — forecast future supply (internal + external labour market)
- **Step 4** — close the gap (hire / develop / outsource / downsize)
- **Demand > Supply** — recruit externally, train, retain, overtime
- **Demand < Supply** — hiring freeze, redeployment, voluntary exits, layoffs
- **Exam move** — frame for PowerTech India (growth) and SolidTech (downsize) cases

### 🔴 Recruitment Process Model · S6 · Lepak & Gowan ★★★★★
*A cycle: plan → strategy → source → method → feedback.*
- **Steps** — HR planning → recruitment strategy → evaluate sources → implement methods → feedback & control
- **Internal sources** — promotions, referrals, internal job postings; low cost, cultural fit, but creates new vacancy
- **External sources** — ads, agencies, social media, campus; fresh skills, costly, less performance data
- **Exam move** — structure any "how should we fill role X" question

### 🔴 Selection Process Model · S6 · Armstrong ★★★★★
*Six steps from application to offer, predicting future performance.*
- **Steps** — application forms → selection tests → interviews → references → physical exam → offer
- **Methods** — structured/unstructured interviews, situational vs behavioural questions, ability/personality tests
- **Predictive validity** — structured interviews > unstructured; ability tests > grades
- **Exam move** — defends *why* the process must be valid and reliable; cite Cappelli (2019) on weak predictors

### 🔵 AMO (Blumberg & Pringle, 1982) · S4 ★★★★
*Performance is multiplicative: a great person in a bad job still fails.*
- **Implication** — job design (the O) can override individual ability/motivation
- **Exam move** — defend a job-redesign intervention over training/hiring

### 🔵 Situational vs Behavioral Interviewing (+ STAR) · S6 · Latham & Sue-Chan (1996) ★★★★
*Two structured-question typologies; STAR structures the answers.*
- **Situational** — "What would you do if…?" → tests intentions/cognitive ability; best for inexperienced candidates
- **Behavioural** — "Tell me about a time when…" → past behaviour predicts future; best for experienced
- **STAR** — Situation → Task → Action → Result
- **Exam move** — pick by candidate experience; STAR boosts validity

### 🔵 RACI Framework · S4 ★★★★
*Roles-and-responsibilities matrix to clarify decision rights.*
- **R**esponsible — does the work
- **A**ccountable — single owner, signs off (only one A per task)
- **C**onsulted — two-way input before the decision
- **I**nformed — one-way notification after
- **Exam move** — apply when redesigning jobs or delegating; eliminates "who owns this?" friction

### 🔵 Job Breadth Interventions · S5 ★★★★
*Four levers to widen or deepen a job.*
- **Enrichment** — vertical: more autonomy, responsibility, challenge
- **Enlargement** — horizontal: more tasks at same level
- **Rotation** — cross-functional movement
- **High-Performance Work Design** — skilled, team-based, supportive systems
- **Exam move** — choose by what's blocking motivation (challenge? variety? skills?)

### 🟣 Stretch Goals / Comfort vs Stretch Zone · S4 · Simons ★★★
*Goal difficulty calibrates span of influence.*
- **Comfort** — attainable, routine; provides stability
- **Stretch** — difficult, near-impossible; drives learning and cross-unit cooperation
- **Exam move** — set stretch goals to widen span of influence in a redesigned role

### 🟣 Selection-Interview Biases · S6 ★★★
*Two named cognitive biases that wreck interview validity.*
- **Contrast effect** — judge a candidate against others just interviewed
- **Similar-to-me effect** — favour candidates resembling the interviewer
- **Exam move** — critique fairness; advocate structured interviews and panel scoring

### 🟣 Employer Brand / EVP · S6 ★★★
*Recruitment = a two-way exchange where the firm sells itself too.*
- **Employer brand** — the firm's reputation as a place to work
- **EVP** — what employees get in return (pay, growth, culture, purpose)
- **Exam move** — explain why employer-of-choice firms attract better applicants

### 🟣 Layoff Selection Criteria · S6 ★★★
*Five bases for choosing who exits.*
- **Seniority** — last in, first out
- **Employer status** — temporary vs core staff
- **Skill** — keep critical capabilities
- **Merit** — performance-based
- **Personal situation** — hardship considerations
- **Exam move** — defend a fair, criterion-based layoff plan (PowerTech India)

# Block 3 · Recruitment, Selection, Socialization, Training & Development

### 🔴 Training Process Model · S10 ★★★★★
*Four-part cyclical model for any effective training program.*
- **Needs Assessment** — organizational, task, person analysis
- **Design** — objectives, lesson plans, content sequencing
- **Implementation** — methods (classroom, on-the-job, e-learning, simulation)
- **Evaluation** — reactions → learning → behaviour change → results (loops back)
- **Exam move** — diagnose why training fails (skipped a step?); structure any "design a training" answer

### 🔴 STAR Method · S9 ★★★★★
*Behavioural-interview answer formula; the "4 P's" for prep.*
- **S**ituation — set the scene briefly
- **T**ask — define the challenge/objective
- **A**ction — *your* specific actions (not "we")
- **R**esult — quantifiable outcome + what you learned
- **4 P's** — Polish résumé, Prepare STARs, Prepare questions, Practice
- **Exam move** — defend why behavioural answers must be specific, personal, measurable

### 🔴 Person–Organization vs Person–Job Fit · S8 ★★★★★
*Two selection criteria with very different consequences.*
- **P–O fit** — alignment with culture/values/norms; CAN be socialized
- **P–J fit** — KSAOs match the job; HARDER to socialize/retrain
- **Trade-off** — over-selecting for P–O fit → homogeneity → reduced diversity/innovation
- **Exam move** — defend the right mix; cite Spencer Stuart for measuring P–O fit

### 🔴 Recruitment & Selection Process · Armstrong (Ch24) · S7–S8 ★★★★★
*End-to-end arc from defining the role to handling problems.*
- **Steps** — define requirements → attract candidates → process applications → select (interviews + tests) → decision → references → offer → onboarding
- **Recruitment problems** — shortages, mismatched expectations, biased screening
- **Exam move** — the spine of any recruitment essay; map each session's content onto a step

### 🔵 Judge et al. (2007) Meta-Analytic Path Model · S8 ★★★★
*The empirical hierarchy of performance predictors.*
- **Strongest predictors** — general mental ability, conscientiousness, experience
- **Mediator** — self-efficacy
- **Exam move** — defend cognitive ability tests + conscientiousness over interviews/grades

### 🔵 Big Five / OCEAN · S8 ★★★★
*Five-factor personality model used as a selection test.*
- **O**penness — curiosity, creativity
- **C**onscientiousness — discipline, reliability → strongest job-perf predictor
- **E**xtraversion — sociability
- **A**greeableness — cooperation, trust
- **N**euroticism (Emotional Stability) — calm under stress
- **Exam move** — pair with Judge et al.; the Fuzzme! sim uses it

### 🔵 Three Phases of Selection · S8 · Fisher, Schoenfeldt & Shaw (2014) ★★★★
*Phase model for executing and validating selection.*
- **Phase 1 — Measurement** — accuracy and consistency of techniques
- **Phase 2 — Decision making** — completeness of candidate data
- **Phase 3 — Evaluation** — assess the process from the org's perspective
- **Exam move** — frame to audit a broken selection system

### 🔵 Spencer Stuart Culture Alignment · S8 ★★★★
*8-style culture map on two axes.*
- **Axes** — independence ↔ interdependence; flexibility ↔ stability
- **Styles** — Learning, Purpose, Caring, Order, Safety, Authority, Results, Enjoyment
- **Exam move** — plot candidate vs org to measure P–O fit; redesign culture by shifting axes

### 🔵 Three Domains of Socialization · S10 ★★★★
*Onboarding works in three layers.*
- **Organization** — values, mission, policies, history
- **Group/Department** — supervisor expectations, peer norms, team expertise
- **Task** — responsibilities, performance expectations, resources/tools
- **Exam move** — diagnose onboarding failures by which domain was skipped

### 🔵 Situational vs Behavioral Questions · S9 ★★★★
*Same as Block 2 framework — typology of structured question types.*
- **Situational** — hypothetical, "What would you do if…?"
- **Behavioral** — past, "Tell me about a time when…"
- **Exam move** — match by candidate experience level

### 🟣 Socialization Strategies (Sequential/Individual/Collective) · S10 ★★★
*Typology of onboarding delivery.*
- **Sequential** — predetermined timing, stages, duration
- **Individual** — one-to-one (e.g. supervisor → new hire)
- **Collective** — group cohort training at start
- **Exam move** — collective builds cohort bonds; individual gives tailored attention

### 🟣 Job Description vs Job Specification · S7 ★★★
*The recruiting starting point — same pairing as Block 2.*
- **Description** — duties, environment, reporting
- **Specification** — KSAOs, qualifications, traits
- **Exam move** — without them, recruitment has no criteria

### 🟣 Internal vs External Recruitment Sources · S7 ★★★
*Source-choice trade-off.*
- **Internal** — promotions, referrals → cheap, culturally aligned, motivating; but creates a new vacancy
- **External** — ads, agencies, social media → fresh skills/ideas; costlier, no track record
- **Exam move** — choose by strategy (build-vs-buy) and position level

### 🟣 Validity & Reliability of Tests · S8 · Armstrong ★★★
*Psychometric requirement for any selection test.*
- **Validity** — does it predict the outcome it claims to?
- **Reliability** — does it give consistent results across raters/times?
- **Test types** — intelligence, personality, aptitude
- **Exam move** — defend dropping a popular-but-invalid test

### 🟣 Structured vs Unstructured Interviews · S8 · Armstrong ★★★
*Validity verdict from the meta-analyses.*
- **Structured** — standardized questions, scoring rubric → highest validity among interviews
- **Unstructured** — chat-style → poor predictor (per Cappelli, 2019)
- **Exam move** — defend a structured + panel + scoring rubric

### 🟣 Employer Brand & EVP · S7 · Armstrong ★★★
*"Employer of choice" framing.*
- **Two-way** — candidates buy *and* sell
- **Exam move** — show that recruitment competitiveness depends on brand

### 🟣 Training vs Development · S10 ★★★
*Conceptual split for learning interventions.*
- **Training** — KSAs for *current* job
- **Development** — preparation for *future*, broader, higher-level roles
- **Exam move** — scope a learning plan; argue for both

### 🟣 Kirkpatrick-style Training Evaluation Levels · S10 ★★★
*Four levels for assessing training impact.*
- **Reactions** — did they like it?
- **Learning** — pre/post knowledge tests
- **Behavior** — applied on the job?
- **Results** — org performance / ROI / cost-benefit
- **Exam move** — push past Level 1 reactions to prove training works

# Block 4 · Performance Management & Appraisal

### 🔴 Performance Management Cycle · S11–S14 · Aguinis et al. (2009) ★★★★★
*PM = continuous process of identifying, measuring and developing performance + aligning it with strategy.*
- **MOA logic** — Motivation × Opportunity × Ability
- **Three pillars** — Identify (what to measure) → Measure (how) → Develop (feedback + growth)
- **Two uses** — administrative (pay, promotion, layoffs) vs developmental (coaching, growth)
- **Exam move** — the organizing model of Block 4; frame any PM design question on this spine

### 🔴 Appraisal vs Management · S11–S12 · Aguinis, Joo & Gottfredson (2011) ★★★★★
*The central distinction. Appraisal = once a year. Management = ongoing.*
- **Appraisal** — annual, HR-driven, past-focused, administrative, form-filling
- **Management** — continuous, line-manager-owned, future-focused, strategic, coaching
- **"Why we hate PM"** — most systems do appraisal, call it management, satisfy nobody
- **Exam move** — diagnose any "PM hate" symptom by which pole the system slid to

### 🔴 Forced Distribution / Relative Ranking · S12 · Vitality Health ★★★★★
*Force raters to allocate employees into a fixed-percentage bell curve.*
- **Fixes** — eliminates leniency/inflation, forces differentiation
- **Risks** — destroys team cohesion, can be unfair in high-talent teams
- **Vitality Health categories** — Top Achiever / Achiever / Low Achiever / Unacceptable / Not Rated
- **Exam move** — defend or attack based on culture + maturity of raters

### 🔴 Vitality Health Revised PM · S12 · Bingham & Beer (case 913-501) ★★★★★
*The case redesign: shift from 13-level absolute ratings to forced-distribution + competencies + SMART.*
- **Old** — 13 absolute rating levels, ratings inflated, no differentiation
- **New** — relative ranking (T/A/L/U/NR) + job-specific competencies + SMART objectives + common January cycle + short/long-term cash + equity
- **Exam move** — the canonical "PM redesign" case; cite for any incentive + rating redesign

### 🔴 360-Degree Feedback · Alex Sander · Aguinis ★★★★★
*Multi-source feedback: supervisor + peers + reports + sometimes customers.*
- **Strengths** — richer picture, surfaces blind spots, signals fairness
- **Risks** — contextually unsafe in low-trust cultures; consumes time
- **Exam move** — defend 360 only where culture supports honest peer feedback (Alex Sander = it didn't)

### 🔵 Three Indicators of Performance · S11 ★★★★
*What can be measured: traits, behaviors, outcomes.*
- **Traits** — enduring characteristics; must fit the job (risky for bias)
- **Behaviors** — observable actions on the job
- **Outcomes** — results delivered
- **Exam move** — defend the right blend (e.g. outcomes alone misses how; behaviors alone misses results)

### 🔵 BARS · S11 ★★★★
*Behaviorally Anchored Rating Scales: each numeric level is anchored to a critical-incident behavior.*
- **Build** — collect critical incidents → scale them → use as anchors
- **Strength** — concrete, behavior-based, low rater drift
- **Cost** — expensive to develop per job
- **Exam move** — cite as the gold-standard subjective method

### 🔵 Management by Objectives (MBO) · Ch10 · S13 ★★★★
*Manager + employee jointly set specific objectives; evaluation = attainment vs objectives.*
- **Process** — joint goal-setting → ongoing review → year-end evaluation
- **Exam move** — outcomes-based PM; pair with [[smart-goals]]

### 🔵 SMART Goals · S13 ★★★★
*Goal-setting acronym.*
- **S**pecific · **M**easurable · **A**ttainable · **R**elevant · **T**ime-bound
- **Exam move** — the operational tool when managing low performers; pair with [[mbo]]

### 🔵 Absolute vs Relative Judgment · S11 · S12 ★★★★
*Two appraisal logics; pick by your differentiation problem.*
- **Absolute** — rate against fixed standards; no comparison
- **Relative** — rank-order or forced-distribute among employees
- **Exam move** — Vitality moved absolute → relative to break rating inflation

### 🟣 Blake & Mouton's Managerial Grid · S13 ★★★
*9×9 grid: concern for people × concern for task.*
- **Five styles** — Impoverished (1,1), Country Club (1,9), Authority-Compliance (9,1), Middle-of-the-Road (5,5), Team Management (9,9)
- **Exam move** — frame coaching-low-performer trade-offs

### 🟣 SBI-Next Feedback Framework · S14 ★★★
*Structured behavior-focused feedback.*
- **S**ituation — when/where it happened
- **B**ehavior — what you observed (no inference)
- **I**mpact — effect on you / team / outcome
- **Next** — agreed next step
- **Exam move** — concrete tool for the McDonald's / peer-feedback role-plays

### 🟣 Rating-Error Typology · Ch10 ★★★
*Systematic cognitive biases that distort subjective ratings.*
- **Halo** — one trait colors all ratings
- **Leniency / Severity** — rater too soft or too hard
- **Central tendency** — clusters everyone in the middle
- **Contrast** — judging vs others just rated
- **Recency** — over-weights last few months
- **Similar-to-me** — favors people like the rater
- **Fix** — frame-of-reference training
- **Exam move** — show why subjective ratings need rater training

### 🟣 Relevance–Deficiency–Contamination · Ch10 · S11 ★★★
*Measurement-quality framework for any performance metric.*
- **Relevant** — captures true performance
- **Deficient** — misses important aspects (false negative)
- **Contaminated** — captures irrelevant variance (false positive)
- **Plus** — reliable, valid, practical
- **Exam move** — audit any KPI with the Venn diagram

### 🟣 Mixed Standard Scale · Ch10 · Blanz & Ghiselli (1972) ★★★
*Mixed good/avg/poor behavioral statements; scored via key to reduce bias.*
- **Exam move** — alternative subjective rating format; flag when standard rating scales drift

### 🟣 Comparative Procedures · Ch10 ★★★
*Family of relative rating methods.*
- **Simple ranking** — order all employees
- **Alternation ranking** — pick best, then worst, alternating
- **Paired comparison** — compare every pair
- **Forced distribution** — bell curve
- **Exam move** — choose by team size and rater patience

### 🟣 Behavioral Observation Scales (BOS) · Ch10 ★★★
*Rate the frequency with which each effective behavior is performed.*
- **Score** — sum of frequencies per behavior
- **Exam move** — variation on BARS, simpler to use day-to-day

### 🟣 Deloitte's Reinvented PM · S11 (HBR video) ★★★
*Real-world redesign: kill ratings; weekly check-ins + four future-focused statements.*
- **The four statements (per employee)**
  - I would always want this person on my team
  - I would give this person the highest possible compensation increase
  - This person is at risk of low performance
  - This person is ready for promotion today
- **Exam move** — cite as practitioner example of moving appraisal → management

# Block 5 · Compensation, Incentives, Benefits & Employment Relations

### 🔴 The Pay Model · S15 · Milkovich, Newman & Gerhart (Ch1) ★★★★★
*The foundational diagnostic: objectives → policy choices → techniques.*
- **Objectives** — efficiency, fairness, ethics, compliance
- **Four policy choices** — internal alignment, external competitiveness, employee contributions, management
- **Techniques** — job analysis, surveys, pay structures, performance-based pay, communication
- **Exam move** — diagnose any pay system as objective-fit + policy-fit + technique-fit

### 🔴 Total Returns / Total Compensation · S15 · Ch1 ★★★★★
*Reward = cash + benefits + relational/psychological returns.*
- **Total Compensation (transactional)** — base, merit/COLA, short- and long-term incentives, benefits
- **Relational/Psychological Returns** — recognition, employment security, challenging work, learning, leadership opportunities
- **Exam move** — argue that pay alone is a thin lever; design the whole "return mix"

### 🔴 Four Criteria of a Competitive Compensation System · S15 · Ch1 ★★★★★
*Operational tests for the Pay Model's policy choices.*
- **Internal equity** — equal pay for jobs of equal worth (via job analysis + evaluation)
- **External equity** — at par with industry (via salary surveys)
- **Performance link** — pay tied to performance
- **Cost/benefit** — affordable, returns measurable
- **Exam move** — score any system on these four; pinpoint which is failing

### 🔴 Pay-for-Performance · S15 · BAG · Zero Wage cases ★★★★★
*Link pay to performance, distinguishing merit increases (permanent) from incentives (variable).*
- **Merit increases** — add to base pay; reward past behavior; permanent → cumulative cost
- **Incentives** — re-earned each period; specified in advance; reward future behavior
- **Exam move** — defend variable pay (incentives) vs sticky merit (BAG Grocers); when to use which

### 🔵 Incentive Effect vs Sorting Effect · S15 · Gerhart & Rynes ★★★★
*Pay shapes performance two ways — through current employees AND who you attract.*
- **Incentive effect** — pay motivates the people you already have
- **Sorting effect** — pay structures attract/retain different *types* of employees (self-selection)
- **Evidence** — 44% productivity rise in glass-installation study, mostly from sorting
- **Exam move** — argue pay design changes *who* you have, not only how hard they work

### 🔵 Types of Incentive Plans · S15 ★★★★
*Classify by level of aggregation.*
- **Individual** — commissions, bonuses, awards, piece-rate
- **Group** — team bonuses, gain-sharing
- **Organization** — profit-sharing, ESOPs, stock options
- **Exam move** — match plan to what behavior you want to incentivize (individual vs team vs firm)

### 🔵 Pay Structure Types · S16 ★★★★
*Three structural shapes for pay grades.*
- **Narrow-graded** — many grades, multiple step progressions
- **Broad-graded** — few grades, long within-grade progression (broadbanding)
- **Job family** — pay based on occupational category, family-specific ranges
- **Exam move** — frame for the salary-negotiation activity; broadbanding fits flatter orgs

### 🔵 Varieties of Capitalism · S16 · Hall & Soskice (2001) ★★★★
*Comparative institutions: why pay/labor practices vary by country.*
- **Coordinated Market Economies (CMEs)** — higher unionization, extensive legislation, institutional collaboration (Germany, Nordics)
- **Liberal Market Economies (LMEs)** — lower unionization, hierarchical coordination, less legislation (US, UK)
- **Exam move** — explain why "the right" pay system is country-specific

### 🟣 Direct vs Indirect Compensation · S15 ★★★
*Maps the full pay mix beyond cash.*
- **Direct** — basic pay, variable pay, profit-sharing
- **Indirect monetary** — health insurance, pension
- **Indirect non-monetary** — telecommuting, flexi-hours, childcare
- **Exam move** — defend the full mix when challenged on "just raise base pay"

### 🟣 Likelihood-to-Unionize Matrix · S16 ★★★
*2×2: benefit of unionizing × ability to organize.*
- **High-High** — blue-collar, public sector → most likely to unionize
- **Low benefit / high ability** — white-collar
- **High benefit / low ability** — gig, temporary workers
- **Low-Low** — self-employed
- **Exam move** — predict where unionization will rise next

### 🟣 Caveat Emptor — Informed Consumer · Ch1 (Circuit City) ★★★
*Three-question critical-appraisal model for any compensation claim.*
- **Useful?** — does the finding apply to my context?
- **Correlation ≠ causation?** — is there a true causal link?
- **Alternatives?** — what other explanations fit the data?
- **Exam move** — apply to any "compensation X drives Y" claim

### 🟣 WorldatWork Total Rewards Model · Ch1 (footnote 28) ★★★
*Industry-standard parallel to Total Returns.*
- **Five buckets** — compensation, benefits, work-life, performance/recognition, development/career
- **Exam move** — cite as practitioner version of [[total-returns]]

# Block 6 · Unions, Collective Bargaining & Labor Relations

### 🔴 Union Wage Effect & Feedback Effects · Pfeffer / Freeman & Medoff ★★★★★
*Unions raise wages — but higher wages don't necessarily lower profits or competitiveness.*
- **Indirect/feedback effects**
  - higher wages attract more skilled workers
  - reduce turnover
  - push firms to economize on labor (capital, training)
  - raise productivity
- **Boundary condition** — union profit-effects matter mainly where firms hold monopoly power
- **Exam move** — defend that the wage-vs-profit trade-off is overstated; cite the feedback mechanisms

### 🔴 Unions & High-Commitment Work Systems · Pfeffer ★★★★★
*Unionization is positively associated with HPWS — and makes them durable.*
- **Mechanism** — contracts lock in training, teams, job security, info sharing → prevent "backsliding"
- **Cases** — Ford, Saturn (GM), Kaiser-Permanente
- **Exam move** — flip the conventional wisdom that unions kill HPWS; unions can entrench them

### 🔵 High Road vs Low Road Strategy · Pfeffer ★★★★
*Two competitive paths; only one is sustainable in advanced economies.*
- **Low road** — compete on low labor costs (unwinnable vs developing economies)
- **High road** — compete on innovation, productivity, brainpower via HPWS
- **Exam move** — argue unionization makes the high road more likely and sustainable

### 🔵 "Management Gets the Union It Deserves" · Pfeffer ★★★★
*The conventional-wisdom claim Pfeffer sets up to dismantle.*
- **Claim** — progressive HR practices forestall unionization; if you're unionized, you failed at HR
- **Pfeffer's challenge** — even good HR doesn't make unions redundant; unions add complementary protections
- **Exam move** — use as the thesis to argue against; the structure of any pro-union essay

### 🔵 Collective Bargaining as Power-Sharing · Pfeffer ★★★★
*Bargaining = institutionalized communication + joint decision-making with front-line staff.*
- **Outcomes when collaborative** — lower hospital mortality (unionized nurses); Kaiser-Permanente partnership
- **Outcomes when adversarial** — gains shrink, both sides lose
- **Exam move** — defend partnership-style labor relations over adversarial bargaining

### 🟣 Union Effect on Wage Distribution · Pfeffer ★★★
*Unions compress the wage structure → less inequality + less discrimination.*
- **Mechanism** — egalitarian ethos needed for collective action
- **Bonus** — wide pay dispersion tends to harm performance
- **Exam move** — answer "what about inequality?" — unions are part of the solution

### 🟣 Collective Bargaining Process · Tepperman's case ★★★
*The structured negotiation arc, from positions to ratification.*
- **Negotiation committee** — union stewards + business agent (union side); management + legal counsel (firm side)
- **Process** — opening positions → bargaining → tentative agreement → ratification vote (majority)
- **Failure path** — if rejected (Tepperman's: 23–4) → renegotiate or strike
- **Exam move** — diagnose why a tentative deal failed ratification (member buy-in?)

- **Exam move** — cite Gerhart & Rynes when challenged "but money doesn't really motivate"

- **Pfeffer** — unions support HPWS, sustain the high road, compress inequality
- **Exam move** — the dialectic of any unions essay
